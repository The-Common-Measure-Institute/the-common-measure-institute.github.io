Analytical approach

Start with what can be measured, not with the preferred narrative.

01

What are the numbers?

Supply, demand, costs, prices, returns, physical quantities and observable outcomes.

02

What are the constraints?

Geology, technology, infrastructure, capital, institutions, policy and time.

03

What are the economics?

Cost curves, cash flows, investment returns, incentives, trade-offs and opportunity costs.

04

What follows?

The implications for markets, companies, investors, governments and public policy.

Publication principles

Evidence should be inspectable.

Common Measure aims to make the chain from evidence to conclusion visible. Where practical, published work identifies its assumptions, sources, uncertainties and limitations rather than hiding them behind a headline conclusion.

  1. 01
    Evidence before narrative.
  2. 02
    Quantification where possible.
  3. 03
    Assumptions made explicit.
  4. 04
    Alternatives compared on a consistent basis.
  5. 05
    Uncertainty and limitations acknowledged.
  6. 06
    Conclusions open to revision as evidence changes.

The Common Measure

Quantify the proposition. Identify the constraints. Test the economics. Examine the alternatives. Then form a view.

Different technologies, projects, policies and market claims become more useful when they are put onto a comparable basis.
